# Public and Historical Figure Extraction

Use this path when the subject is known primarily through published works rather than private workplace artifacts.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. works authored or spoken by the subject;
2. complete transcripts, recordings, letters, papers, essays, or books;
3. contemporary records and documented cases;
4. rigorous biographies or scholarly analyses;
5. reputable summaries for orientation only.

Quote aggregators and unsourced anecdotes are discovery aids, not evidence. Trace quotations to a primary or reliable source before using them as rules.

## Acquire and normalize sources

For each source:

1. record full bibliographic information;
2. preserve the original file or stable reference when permitted;
3. extract or transcribe text using available tools;
4. record the extraction method and known quality limits;
5. keep page, chapter, section, timestamp, or paragraph anchors;
6. check a sample against the original to detect extraction errors.

Do not include complete copyrighted works in the generated echo. Derive principles and retain only short necessary excerpts plus citations.

## Map document structure

Identify front matter, table of contents, chapters or sections, notes, appendices, and repeated headers. Table-of-contents headings are not body evidence. Search for later occurrences and verify surrounding text.

When formatting is inconsistent, use several signals:

- known section titles;
- page or timestamp markers;
- typographic or transcript boundaries;
- topic transitions;
- repeated header patterns.

## Find decision-relevant material

Search for clusters of terms associated with:

- principles: should, must, never, always, rule, principle;
- decisions: decide, choose, reject, approve, judgment;
- uncertainty: experiment, test, risk, reversible, evidence;
- organization: delegate, authority, coordinate, responsibility;
- evaluation: useful, feasible, sound, quality, complete;
- failure and revision: mistake, changed, learned, abandoned, corrected.

Keyword matches only locate candidates. Read the full surrounding argument before deriving a rule.

## Extract context windows and cases

For a candidate passage, capture enough context to answer:

- What problem was being discussed?
- Was the statement descriptive, prescriptive, rhetorical, or ironic?
- Did the subject act consistently with it?
- Was the result reported?
- What historical conditions limit transfer?

Cases are especially valuable because they expose conditions and tradeoffs. Keep the situation, decision, rationale, result, and derived lesson separate.

## Analyze voice carefully

Sample multiple source types and periods before modeling expression. Published prose, prepared talks, interviews, and private letters can differ substantially.

Characterize:

- sentence rhythm and argument structure;
- use of analogy, anecdote, humor, and questions;
- directness and qualification;
- recurring rhetorical moves;
- preferred scale of explanation;
- changes over time.

Do not overfit famous quotations. A recognizable echo should arise from reasoning patterns more than catchphrases.

## Infer demonstrated rules cautiously

A historical source can support both:

- **Quoted rule:** the subject states a principle directly.
- **Inferred rule:** a documented decision repeatedly demonstrates a pattern.

Label them differently. For an inferred rule, cite the cases and explain the smallest supported generalization. Do not infer private motives when observable decision criteria suffice.

## Handle changing views

Public figures often revise positions. Record:

- the date or period of each view;
- whether the change is explicit;
- the evidence or event associated with the revision;
- which period the desired echo should model.

Do not collapse decades of work into one timeless position without acknowledging evolution.

## Separate historical method from current truth

The echo may apply a durable method or principle, but it must independently verify current facts. Historical scientific claims, social assumptions, technologies, laws, and institutional details may be obsolete or harmful.

## Corrective review

Because the subject may be unavailable to correct the model:

- make uncertainty prominent;
- seek multiple independent sources;
- invite knowledgeable user corrections;
- preserve disputed interpretations in `meta.json`;
- avoid asserting that the figure would definitely endorse a modern conclusion.
