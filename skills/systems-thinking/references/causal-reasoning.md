# Causal reasoning boundary

Systems thinking proposes mechanisms. A temporal association, dependency edge, or plausible loop does not establish a causal effect.

For each consequential causal claim:

- Separate directly observed events from inferred connections.
- State assumptions, time order, delays, and the population or operating conditions where the claim applies.
- Consider common causes, reverse causation, selection effects, and measurement changes.
- Predict evidence that would differ under an alternative explanation.
- Seek contradictory observations and record checks and results separately.

A code trace can establish that a retry path exists. It cannot alone establish how much that path increases production latency. A test can establish behavior under its conditions without estimating a real-world effect.

When the task requires causal effect estimation or discovery from observational or experimental data, use a separate causal-inference skill if one is available. Supply the question, candidate structure, variables, timing, assumptions, and data provenance. Bring its evidence and limitations back to the verification playbook.

No causal-inference skill is bundled or required here. If unavailable, state what data, identification assumptions, and specialized analysis are needed. Do not substitute a structural story for an estimated or identified effect. Do not expand a systems review into a quantitative research project unless requested.
