# Choose an intervention

What is the smallest change that tests the model and improves the objective?

## Load

Read [constraints and bottlenecks](../principles/constraints-and-bottlenecks.md), [boundaries and purpose](../principles/boundaries-and-purpose.md), and [path dependence and reversibility](../principles/path-dependence-and-reversibility.md). Add feedback and delay principles when they affect response; add operational burden or incentives when costs move between actors.

## Procedure

1. Start from a current model, objective, evidence, and likely constraint. If absent, use [understand a system](understand-system.md) or [diagnose a problem](diagnose-problem.md) only as far as needed to establish them.
2. Identify leverage points that alter the mechanism rather than just its symptoms. Consider deletion, clearer ownership, existing capabilities, bounded accumulation, information flow, interfaces, and incentives before adding management or platforms.
3. Compare candidate changes, including deferral when evidence is weak. Predict first-order effects, second-order effects, displaced bottlenecks, and who bears the costs.
4. Choose a small, reversible experiment. Define expected result, success and failure measures, observation window, rollback or stopping condition, and next review point. Account for delays before judging the result.
5. Use [verify a system model](verify-system-model.md) when the recommendation depends on an uncertain causal claim. Keep proposed experiments distinct from executed work and respect existing authorization.

## Deliver

Return the constraint, candidate leverage points and tradeoffs, recommended smallest intervention, what to defer, predicted effects, risks, measurement that could falsify the model, and review or stopping conditions. Do not claim an intervention is effective before evidence supports it.
